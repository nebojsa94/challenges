## Honest Assessment

You've done this to yourself before. 5 weeks for a marathon was "not enough" and you finished in 4:14. So let's skip the part where I tell you it's impossible and get to the actual math.

**The good news is structural:**

1. **Your run is already done.** You are a 4:14 marathoner who runs 10km at conversational pace every week. A flat half marathon off the bike is completely within you. Most first-time 70.3 athletes fear the run — for you it's the victory lap.
2. **The bike is flat and forgiving.** 90km on flat closed city roads at a sustainable effort is an endurance problem, not a skill problem — and endurance is the thing you demonstrably have. Your aerobic engine (built by the marathon block) transfers directly.
3. **The race is in your backyard.** The swim is in Sava Lake at Ada Ciganlija — the lake you live next to. You can train on the *exact* race course, in the *exact* water, every week. Nobody else on the start line gets that.
4. **Generous cutoffs.** Swim: 1h10 from your start. Bike: 5h30 total race time. Finish: 8h30. The math below shows you have enormous margin everywhere except one place.

**The bad news is singular but serious:**

**You cannot currently swim 1900m.** Your longest swim ever is 650m. The swim is the one discipline where "grit through it" does not work — fatigue in deep water is a safety problem, not a suffering problem. This entire plan is therefore built around one non-negotiable priority: **swim three times a week, every week, no exceptions.** Everything else can flex. The swims cannot.

Swim fitness is also the fastest fitness to build — it's technique- and frequency-limited, not engine-limited, and your engine is already big. Going from 650m to 1900m continuous in 4 weeks at 3 swims/week is a well-trodden path. With a wetsuit (23°C water = wetsuit legal) you get free buoyancy, and breaststroke recovery is legal in IRONMAN races.

### Goal

**Finish.** Target window: **6:30–7:15**. The plan's pacing gets you home around 6:45 with margin on every cutoff. A blown swim still leaves you ~7:15. This is a survival plan with a comfortable buffer — the only DNF scenarios are a swim you didn't train for (this plan fixes that), a mechanical you can't repair (we prepare for that), or a nutrition failure (we rehearse that).

### Risk Acknowledgment

- The swim is a genuine risk until Week 4's 1900m rehearsal proves otherwise. **Decision point: September 5.** If you cannot swim 1900m continuous (any stroke mix) in Ada by then, we have an honest conversation about the start line.
- Never swim alone in open water. Ada has lifeguarded zones — use them, or swim with a buddy and a swim buoy.
- Riding 90km means hours on Belgrade-area roads in traffic during training. Ride defensively, lights on, no headphones.
- Triple-sport load on a body that isn't used to it: niggles will appear. Sharp pain = stop. Arriving underprepared beats not arriving.

## Course Profile: IRONMAN 70.3 Belgrade

- **Swim (1.9km):** Sava Lake, Ada Ciganlija. Closed, protected, flat water — no current, no waves, no salt. Water temp ~23°C in September → **wetsuit legal** (limit 24.5°C). Rolling start: you seed yourself by expected time, cross the timing mat, and your personal 1:10 clock starts. Seed at the back — clean water, no washing machine.
- **Bike (90km):** Two flat loops on wide city avenues, through the Ada bridge area. Fast, closed roads, no real climbing. Wind on exposed river sections is the only terrain feature. **Draft-illegal race:** keep 12m behind the rider ahead or pass within 25 seconds.
- **Run (21.1km):** Flat circuit around Sava Lake — the exact paths you already run. Shaded wooded sections, aid stations at start/mid/finish of each lap.
- **Weather:** September in Belgrade: 14–27°C. Race morning likely 15–18°C, afternoon warm and possibly sunny on the exposed lake loop — sunscreen and a cap.

## 5-Week Training Plan

### Philosophy

- **Swim frequency over everything.** 3 swims/week × 5 weeks = 14 swims between you and race day. Each one matters more than any ride or run.
- **The bike is built on your engine.** Long rides grow from 40km (done) to ~85km. Effort always conversational. You learn to eat and drink *while riding* — that's the actual skill being trained.
- **The run is maintained, not built.** Two runs a week, one of them off the bike. Your marathon base carries you.
- **Brick workouts** (bike → immediate run) every weekend from Week 2. The first 2km off the bike feel like someone replaced your legs — you need to have felt that before race day.
- **One full rest day per week minimum.** With 8 sessions/week across three sports, sleep is a training session.
- **Essential sessions** are marked ★. If life happens and you must cut, cut anything unmarked first. Never cut a ★.

## Race Day Plan

### Pacing

| Segment | Target | Effort rule |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| Swim | 48–55min | So easy it feels embarrassing. Long strokes, sight every 8–10, breaststroke whenever you need to reset. You cannot win the race in the water, you can only lose it. |
| T1 | ~6min | Walk if the heart rate is high. Helmet ON before touching the bike. |
| Bike 0–20km | HR ≤ 140 | Everyone will pass you. Let them. They're eating your dinner now; you'll eat theirs at km 15 of the run. |
| Bike 20–85km | HR 135–145, ~26–27km/h | Metronome mode. Eat/drink on the timer, not on feeling. |
| Bike 85–90km | Spin easy, high cadence | Light gear, wake the legs up for the run. |
| T2 | ~4min | Rack bike, helmet off AFTER racking. Socks, run shoes, race belt, cap, 2 gels. |
| Run 0–5km | 6:20/km — deliberately slow | Brick legs disappear around km 3–4. Do not trust how "fine" you feel. |
| Run 5–18km | 6:00–6:10/km | Walk every aid station. Coke from ~km 12 onward is rocket fuel. |
| Run 18–21.1km | Whatever is left | Empty it. The finish chute at Ada, in your city, on your lake. |

**Projected finish: ~6:40–6:55.** Sub-6:30 if the day is kind. Anything under 8:30 gets the same medal and the same title: IRONMAN 70.3 finisher.

### Race Nutrition (rehearse ALL of this on Sep 5)

| When | What |
|------|------|
| 3h before | Big breakfast: toast + jam, banana, coffee. 500ml water. Stop eating 90min out. |
| Pre-swim (15min) | 1 gel + few sips of water |
| Bike, every 20min | Drink (alternate water / electrolyte). ~2 bottles total, grab more at aid stations. |
| Bike, every 40min | 1 gel or half a bar → target 60–70g carbs/hour. ~5 feeds total on the bike. **The run is fueled on the bike.** If you under-eat riding, the run collapses. |
| T2 | 1 gel going out |
| Run, every ~5km | 1 gel (carry 3) + whatever the course offers. Coke in the second half. |
| Total | ~9–10 gels/bars across the day. Buy 15 of ONE brand now, train with them. |

### The Rules That Get People DQ'd

- **Helmet buckled before you unrack the bike. Helmet stays on until the bike is racked.** The #1 rookie DQ.
- **No drafting:** 12m gap (about 6 bike lengths). Pass in under 25s. Getting passed? Drop back out of the zone.
- **No headphones. Ever. Anywhere on course.**
- Race number: back on the bike, front on the run (race belt = just rotate it).
- Mount line: walk the bike out of transition, mount AFTER the line.
- Littering outside aid station zones = penalty. Stuff wrappers in your suit.

## Swimming: The Actual Priority (A Mini-Guide)

Because this is the part you can't grit:

1. **The panic is normal and it's not fitness.** Open water + no wall + can't see bottom = adrenaline = short breath = feels like drowning while your muscles are fine. The fix is exposure (hence 4+ OW sessions) and one trick: **exhale fully underwater**. Panic is almost always breath-stacking.
2. **Slow down.** Your instinct will be to fight the water. Gliding beats thrashing. A 2:45/100m relaxed swimmer beats a 2:15 swimmer who blows up at 800m.
3. **Sighting:** every 8–10 strokes, eyes up like a crocodile mid-stroke, pick the landmark, head back down. Practice from Week 2 or you'll swim 2200m on race day.
4. **Breaststroke is legal.** So is backstroke. So is stopping to tread water. The only illegal things are holding a buoy/kayak *to make progress* or standing on the bottom to walk. Your race stroke can be "freestyle until bored, breaststroke to sight and breathe, repeat" — thousands finish exactly that way.
5. **Wetsuit = cheat mode.** Legs float, body warm, buoyant even at rest. Swim in it at least 3 times before race day (it changes your stroke). Vaseline/Body Glide on the neck.
6. **If budget and time allow ONE luxury: two swim lessons in Week 1.** A coach watching your breathing for 45 minutes will save you more time than every other session combined.

## Recovery (Same Rules as the Marathon, But More)

- 8+ hours sleep. You're now training ~9h/week on top of a job. Sleep is when the adaptation happens.
- Eat like it's your job. You'll be burning 700–900 extra calories/day in big weeks. Underfueling in training = injuries + garbage sessions.
- The marathon strength circuit (glute bridges, calf raises, planks) — 2×/week through Week 3, then stop.
- Foam roll after runs; shake out shoulders after swims.
- **Niggle protocol:** anything sharp → 2 days off that sport (swim instead — it's zero impact and it's the priority anyway). The plan has enough margin to absorb it.

### Warning Signs — Stop and Reassess
- Sharp pain that changes your stroke/pedal/gait
- Resting HR elevated 8–10bpm for 2+ mornings (overreaching — take a rest day)
- Dread before *every* session (one skipped session costs nothing; burnout costs the race)

## The Unknown Unknowns, Triathlon Edition

1. **Race week admin is a discipline.** Athlete check-in (Fri), race briefing, bike check-in (Sat), transition bags, body marking, timing chip. Read the athlete guide twice. Set a checklist. Half of first-timer stress is logistics, not fitness.
2. **Memorize transition.** Walk it Saturday: swim-in → your rack → bike-out. Count the rows. After 1900m of swimming your brain is porridge and 3000 identical bikes look identical.
3. **Wetsuit removal is a skill.** Practice twice: suit half down as you exit the water, step on it, rip it off. Or accept 90 extra seconds — you have the margin.
4. **Goggle kicks happen.** If someone's heel finds your face: roll to your back, fix, carry on. This is why you seeded at the back.
5. **You will want to hammer the first 20km of the bike.** Post-swim adrenaline + flat road + crowds = the classic 70.3 suicide. HR cap 140. Boring wins.
6. **Peeing.** 6+ hours of drinking. Portaloos exist in transition and at aid stations. Pros pee on the bike; you may simply stop like a civilized person.
7. **Chafing, new locations:** wetsuit neck, saddle region, tri-suit shoulder seams. Body Glide everything.
8. **Feet:** wet feet into bike shoes/sneakers for 3.5h, then a half marathon. Small towel in T1, socks for the bike (yes, it costs 20 seconds; it saves your feet).
9. **The 2:45am brain:** race morning starts ~4:45–5:15 wake-up for a ~7–8am start. Practice the early alarm once (Sep 5 rehearsal — do it at race wake-up time).
10. **Mantra still applies.** Yours got you up the Hotel Moskva hill. The lake loop's km 15 will need it again. "I chose this."
11. **Post-race:** you'll be uselessly, happily broken for 3–4 days. And you'll be an IRONMAN 70.3 finisher 5 months after being a marathoner, 6 months after being a guy who hadn't run in a year. Plan the celebration in advance.
