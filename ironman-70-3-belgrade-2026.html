You cannot currently swim 1900m. Longest ever: 650m. The swim is the one discipline you can't grit through — deep-water fatigue is a safety problem, not a suffering problem. The entire plan hangs on one rule: swim 3× per week, every week, no exceptions. Everything else can flex.

🌊 The Swim: What Actually Matters

Panic is breath, not fitness

Open water + no wall + dark bottom = adrenaline = short breath = "drowning" feeling while your muscles are fine. Fix: exhale fully underwater, and exposure — hence 4+ open-water sessions before race day.

Slow down to speed up

Gliding beats thrashing. A relaxed 2:45/100m swimmer beats a 2:15 swimmer who blows up at 800m. Your race pace should feel embarrassingly easy.

Sighting

Every 8–10 strokes: eyes up like a crocodile, find the buoy, head down. Practice from Week 2 or you'll swim 2200m on race day.

Breaststroke is legal

So is backstroke, so is treading water to reset. Illegal: making progress holding a kayak, or walking on the bottom. "Freestyle until bored, breaststroke to breathe, repeat" finishes thousands of 70.3 swims every year.

Wetsuit = cheat mode

Legs float, body warm, buoyant even at rest. Swim in it 3+ times before race day — it changes your stroke. Body Glide on the neck.

The one luxury worth buying

Two swim lessons in Week 1. A coach watching your breathing for 45 minutes saves more time than every other session combined.

The Rules That DQ Rookies

  • Helmet buckled before unracking. Helmet on until re-racked. The #1 first-timer DQ.
  • No drafting: 12m gap (~6 bike lengths), pass within 25s.
  • No headphones. Ever. Instant DQ.
  • Number on your back for the bike, front for the run — race belt, just rotate it.
  • Mount AFTER the mount line; dismount BEFORE the dismount line.
  • Litter only in aid-station zones.

? The Unknown Unknowns, Triathlon Edition

1. Race week admin is a discipline

Check-in, briefing, bike check-in, body marking, timing chip. Read the athlete guide twice. Half of first-timer stress is logistics, not fitness.

2. 3000 bikes look identical

After 1900m your brain is porridge. Memorize your rack row Saturday. Landmark, count, repeat.

3. Wetsuit removal is a skill

Practice twice: peel to waist exiting the water, step and rip. Or accept +90s — you have the margin.

4. A heel finds your goggles

Roll to your back, fix, carry on. This is why you seeded at the back.

5. The first 20km of the bike

Post-swim adrenaline + flat road = the classic blow-up. HR cap 140. Boring wins races.

6. Peeing is logistics

6+ hours of drinking. Portaloos in transition and at every aid station. Plan for one stop, lose 90 seconds, stay human.

7. New chafing frontiers

Wetsuit neck, saddle region, tri-suit seams. Body Glide everything twice.

8. Wet feet for 6 hours

Small towel in T1, socks for the bike. 20 seconds spent, feet saved for the half marathon.

9. The 4:45am brain

Practice the race wake-up once — the Sep 5 rehearsal starts at race time for exactly this reason.

10. Run km 15 will ask why

Same voice from marathon km 32. Same answer: "I chose this." It worked on the Hotel Moskva hill; it works on the lake loop.

11. Post-race

Happily broken for 3–4 days. Then a finisher medal on the shelf and a dangerous thought: "what's next?" (See: the hub page has a slot open.)
